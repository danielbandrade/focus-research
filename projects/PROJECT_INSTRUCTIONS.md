## 4.2 Final Report Structure (6–8 pages)

### 4.2.1 Format of the report

The report will have the format of an article.

1. **Abstract:** Summarize the key idea of the manuscript, results, and conclusion.
2. **Introduction:** Research question and motivation.
3. **Methods:** Data characterization, simulation design, algorithms used, parameters.
4. **Results:** Simulation outputs, convergence diagnostics, uncertainty quantification.
5. **Discussion:** Interpretation, limitations, future work.
6. **References:** Proper citations.
7. **Appendix:** Eventual additional descriptions of methods. Include an explanation about how AI was used.
8. **Implementation:** Key R code (in appendix or as supplementary material).

### 4.2.2 A procedure for preparing the report

**Deadline:** 12.18.26

Suggestions about how to prepare the report.

1. **Data selection:** Select the data for performing the analysis (it is better to be related to your research project, if your advisor agrees). Use at least three numeric features.
2. **Data description:** Describe the data, for example:
    1. How was the data acquired?
    2. What are the limitations of the acquisition methods?
    3. How is the data organized?
    4. Are there missing values?
    5. Is the data numerical, categorical, mixed, structured, or unstructured?
    6. How many features are you expecting to analyze?
3. **Data analysis:** Design a script for analyzing the data.
    1. Elaborate an analysis of the data based on the contents already discussed in class.
    2. Employ a non-parametric analysis based on the empirical CDF.
    3. Compute summary quantities (mean, median, quantiles, correlations, etc.).
    4. Represent your results in graphs (histograms, bar graphs, box plots, empirical CDF, dispersion, etc.).
    5. Do the histograms or bar graphs have a familiar shape?
    6. Can you infer μ and σ for each quantitative feature?
    7. Can you perform a PCA analysis?
4. **Writing:**
    1. Collect the results.
    2. Present and organize your results in subsections (graphs, tables, numbers, data characteristics, etc.).
    3. Present the methods (theoretical and computational) used to obtain the results. Present your complete description of the data.
    4. Describe the presented results.
    5. Discuss the results (interconnect different results aiming to build a synthesis idea, make comparisons between different methods, consider the computational performance, and take the employed methods into consideration while formulating your discussion).
    6. Ask new questions that may drive the generation of additional results.
    7. Keep iterating on the previous steps.
    8. Perform an analysis of the results aiming to synthesize the key idea of your manuscript.
    9. Work on the introduction to introduce the problem being solved in your report.
    10. Work on the conclusion.
    11. Work on the Abstract and Title.
